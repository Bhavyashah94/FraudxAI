Review of Literature • Paper 1 / 4

SHAP-Based Feature Selection for Fraud Labeling

M. A. Walauskis & T. M. Khoshgoftaar
IEEE Access, Vol. 13, July 2025 • Florida Atlantic University
Core Problem

The High Cost of Manual Fraud Labeling

In banking, transactions arrive in massive streams, but almost none are labeled as fraud. Investigating flagged alerts takes days and requires costly human review, leaving real-time systems severely starved of labeled training data.

Their Methodology

Unsupervised SHAP Feature Ranking

Rather than using SHAP only as a post-hoc explainer, they used SHAP to rank features on 284,807 card transactions. They selected the top-ranked features (Amount, V13, V12) to guide an automated unsupervised labeling algorithm without manual annotation.

Key Findings & Research Gaps
✔ What Worked
  • Better Fraud Labels: Top 3 and top 5 SHAP feature subsets generated higher quality labels (measured by Matthews Correlation & Precision) than using all 29 features.
  • Outperformed Baselines: Significantly beat standard unsupervised anomaly detection (Isolation Forest) on imbalanced card data.
⚠ Critical Gaps for Our Project
  • Masked PCA (Zero Semantic Context): Tested on Kaggle data where features are anonymized components (V1–V28). Telling an investigator that "V13 is suspicious" provides zero real-world payment meaning.
  • No Ground Truth for Explanations: The paper proved that SHAP finds fraud labels, but had no way to verify whether the feature rankings themselves were truthful or distorted by correlation.
Takeaway for FraudxAI: We need unmasked domain payment features and a benchmark where ground-truth explanation causes are known.
